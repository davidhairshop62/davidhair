Key Trichoscopic Markers in Frontal Fibrosing Alopecia
Diagnostic Criteria Reference
Peripilar / Perifollicular Casts

Tubular hyperkeratotic collars encircling the base of hair shafts along the active marginal hairline, representing lichenoid infundibular inflammation.

Loss of Follicular Ostia

Complete absence of follicular openings, yielding a distinctively smooth, uniform, porcelain-white cicatricial band devoid of vellus hairs.

Perifollicular Erythema

Subtle to prominent pink-red flush encircling active follicles at the borderline, signifying active lichen planopilaris-type lymphocytic infiltrate.

Predominance of Single-Hair Grafts

Marked reduction of multi-hair follicular units (follicular grouping loss), often leaving solitary isolated terminal strands ("Lonely Hair Sign").

Differential Diagnosis Context for Specialists

FFA must be distinguished from Traction Alopecia (which retains follicular openings and often shows a "fringe sign"), Female Pattern Hair Loss (preserving the frontal edge with central widening), and Classic Lichen Planopilaris (which tends to produce patchy vertex cicatricial plaques rather than a band-like frontal regression).
